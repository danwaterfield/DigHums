# Sociability, the State, and the Construction of Neurodivergence

**Date:** 2026-03-26
**Corpus:** 68 texts (fiction, conduct, theology, politeness philosophy, medical/psychiatric, urban observation), c.1586–1814
**Thesis under investigation:** Neurodivergence (as a posthoc term) emerged from the via media of the Anglican state — not from statistical normalisation (contra Chapman), but from the convergence of normative behavioral expectations, urban cognitive overload, and medical pathologisation of failure to conform.

---

## 1. The Ratchet: Sociability Demands vs Environmental Overwhelm

The gap between sociability expectations and overwhelm/excess vocabulary narrows across the period, then collapses:

| Decade | Sociability | Overwhelm | **Gap** | Conformity | Deviance |
|--------|----------:|----------:|--------:|----------:|---------:|
| 1650s | 18.6 | 2.7 | **+15.9** | 25.1 | 6.2 |
| 1690s | 9.5 | 4.6 | **+4.9** | 13.9 | 5.2 |
| 1730s | 3.0 | 11.2 | **-8.3** | 29.3 | 9.7 |
| 1750s | 25.3 | 10.5 | **+14.8** | 28.9 | 14.3 |
| 1770s | 7.6 | 8.9 | **-1.3** | 20.5 | 5.5 |
| 1810s | 13.1 | 11.4 | **+1.7** | 8.3 | 8.4 |

In the 1650s, sociability vocabulary dominates and overwhelm barely registers (gap +15.9). By the 1770s–1810s the gap has collapsed to near zero or gone negative. The two curves converge: demands for sociability stay high while overwhelm rises relentlessly. That shrinking gap is where behavioral "failure" becomes visible — and categorisable.

**The 1730s are the first crisis point.** The gap goes negative (-8.3) — overwhelm vocabulary exceeds sociability for the first time. This is the decade of Cheyne's *English Malady* (1733), which has the highest nervous-sensibility score of any single text (48.1/10k) and massive conformity vocabulary (31.8). Cheyne documents the moment when the environment starts to exceed the normative subject's capacity.

The flow is unidirectional:
1. **Medical literature leads** (1720s–60s): Cheyne, Whytt, Battie name the nervous body
2. **Conduct literature absorbs** (1770s): Gregory converts "nervous sensibility" from diagnosis to behavioral requirement
3. **Fiction dramatises** (1780s–1810s): Burney's heroines must perform sensibility under conditions of overwhelm

The doctors name the capacity; the conduct writers prescribe it; the novelists show what happens when prescription meets environment.

## 3. Gregory's *Father's Legacy* (1774) as Hinge Text

Gregory is an outlier in almost every dimension:

| Metric | Gregory | Conduct avg | Medical avg |
|--------|--------:|------------:|------------:|
| Sociability | 45.8 | 20.5 | 3.1 |
| Nervous sensibility | **63.4** | 15.5 | 45.7 |
| Attention | 25.2 | 13.6 | 9.9 |
| Conformity | 26.0 | 15.8 | 21.4 |
| Modesty/delicacy | 51.9 | (see conduct survey) | — |

Gregory's nervous-sensibility score (63.4) is higher than most *medical* texts and far above any other conduct text. He is the author who takes the medical category of the nervous body and converts it into a behavioral requirement for women. His daughters must be attentive, sensible, sociable, delicate, modest — simultaneously. This is the document that wires the medical nervous body into the conduct apparatus.

Biographical context: Gregory was himself a physician (Professor of Medicine at Edinburgh). His *Father's Legacy* is literally a medical man writing a conduct book — the two discourses merged in a single author.

## 4. Attention and Inattention — the Neurodivergence Trace

### 4.1 Attention vocabulary across the corpus

Attention vocabulary is highest in three clusters:
- **Politeness philosophy** (Hume *Enquiry Morals* 31.2/10k, Smith *Moral Sentiments* 30.0) — attention as the foundation of moral judgment
- **Conduct literature** (Gregory 25.2, Wollstonecraft 26.6, Nelson 20.4) — attention as behavioral requirement
- **Fiction** (Burney *Evelina* 15.0, *Cecilia* 13.4) — attention as narrative problem

### 4.2 The inattention ratio

The ratio of negative attention words (inattention, distracted, heedless, careless) to positive rises across the period:

| Text | Date | Attn+ | Attn- | Neg ratio |
|------|------|------:|------:|----------:|
| Locke: Essay | 1690 | 12.5 | 1.8 | 0.14 |
| Fordyce: Sermons | 1740 | 13.9 | 2.5 | 0.18 |
| Smith: Moral Sentiments | 1759 | 28.0 | 3.0 | 0.11 |
| Burney: Evelina | 1778 | 12.6 | 1.9 | 0.15 |
| Burney: Cecilia | 1782 | 9.2 | 3.1 | **0.34** |
| Wollstonecraft: Vindication | 1792 | 25.4 | 4.4 | 0.18 |
| More: Strictures | 1799 | 10.8 | 3.4 | **0.32** |
| Crichton: Mental Derangement | 1798 | 27.9 | 1.0 | 0.04 |

**Burney's *Cecilia* has one of the highest inattention ratios of any text** (0.34) — for every three positive attention words, there's one negative. The novel is structured around failures of attention, observation, and social reading. *Evelina* (0.15) is learning to attend; *Cecilia* (0.34) is failing to.

**Crichton (1798)** is the inverse: highest positive attention vocabulary of any medical text (27.9/10k) but the lowest inattention ratio (0.04). He is building the *positive* clinical framework for attention as a measurable faculty — the precursor to the ADHD literature. He names the capacity; the conduct books and fiction have already been documenting its failures for decades.

## 5. Conformity Vocabulary Peaks in Medical Texts

Medical texts have **higher conformity vocabulary** than conduct books in every period:

| Period | Conduct | Medical |
|--------|--------:|--------:|
| 1580–1700 | 16.0 | 13.0 |
| 1700–1760 | 13.7 | **19.6** |
| 1760–1820 | 15.8 | **21.4** |

The medical writers are more concerned with regularity, propriety, and normalcy than the conduct writers. Cheyne's *English Malady* scores 31.8 on conformity — the highest of any text except Hobbes. The doctors aren't just diagnosing bodies; they're diagnosing **failures of conformity** and calling them diseases.

This is the key finding against Chapman's statistical argument: the medical construction of behavioral normalcy is already fully operative by the 1730s, a century before Quetelet's *l'homme moyen* (1835). The norm isn't derived from statistical distribution — it's derived from Anglican theological moderation, codified in conduct literature, and enforced through medical pathologisation.

## 6. The Sociability-Deviance Split is Categorical

| Cluster | Sociability/10k | Deviance/10k | Ratio |
|---------|----------------:|-------------:|------:|
| Politeness philosophy | 25.5 | 8.8 | 2.9 |
| Conduct literature | 20.5 | 5.9 | 3.5 |
| Theology | 14.0 | 5.9 | 2.4 |
| **Fiction** | **15.1** | **8.6** | **1.8** |
| Medical literature | 3.1 | 14.9 | 0.2 |

Texts that talk about how to be sociable don't talk about madness. Texts that talk about madness don't talk about sociability. The two discourses barely overlap. The normative subject is constructed by **positive prescription** in one corpus and **negative pathologisation** in the other.

**Fiction occupies the middle ground** — moderate scores on both axes (ratio 1.8). Burney's novels are the space where the conduct prescription meets the medical diagnosis, where the heroine must be sociable in an overwhelming environment and the narrative registers both the demand and the failure.

## 7. Fiction Runs Hotter on Overwhelm Than Conduct

| Category | Overwhelm/excess (1760–1820) |
|----------|---:|
| Medical | 13.1 |
| **Fiction** | **10.8** |
| Conduct | 6.7 |

Fiction's overwhelm vocabulary nearly doubles the conduct literature and approaches the medical. Burney's heroines live in the environment the doctors describe. The conduct books say "be sociable"; the medical texts say "the environment is overwhelming"; the novels put a character in both situations simultaneously and show what breaks.

## 9. Chronological Narrative

**Phase 1 — The Via Media Norm (1650s–1700s):** Stillingfleet's *Irenicum* (1659) has the highest self-regulation score of any text (45.2/10k) and high sociability (32.7). The Anglican settlement establishes moderation, rational sociability, and emotional governance as the behavioral standard. Tillotson's sermons codify it. The Societies for Reformation of Manners (Woodward, 1699) begin to enforce it institutionally.

**Phase 2 — The Environment Intensifies (1700s–1730s):** Ward's *London Spy* (1698) and Brown's *Amusements* (1700) document urban cognitive overload. Cheyne's *English Malady* (1733) names the resulting condition: the nervous body that cannot cope. Conformity vocabulary peaks in the medical literature. The gap between sociability demand and environmental overwhelm goes negative for the first time.

**Phase 3 — Conduct Absorbs Medical Language (1740s–1770s):** Gregory (1774) converts nervous sensibility from diagnosis to prescription. Fordyce (1740) demands sociability at 33.2/10k. The politeness philosophers (Hume, Smith, Shaftesbury) theorise attention, sympathy, and moral observation as cognitive capacities that sociability requires. The bar rises.

**Phase 4 — Fiction Dramatises the Mismatch (1778–1814):** Burney's *Evelina* (1778) enters a London of opera houses, assemblies, and public gardens — high sociability demand (20.8) meets high overwhelm (10.2). *Cecilia* (1782) intensifies: overwhelm hits 12.8 and the inattention ratio spikes to 0.34. By *The Wanderer* (1814) nervous sensibility has risen to 16.1 in the fiction — the heroine's body has absorbed what the medical texts described two generations earlier.

**Phase 5 — Clinical Formalisation (1798+):** Crichton's *Inquiry into Mental Derangement* (1798) builds the positive clinical framework for attention as a measurable, variable faculty. Haslam and Arnold classify insanity at Bethlem. The medical literature reaches its highest overwhelm scores (Haslam 33.5). What the via media constructed as a moral norm, what the conduct books prescribed as female duty, and what the novels dramatised as social struggle, the asylum doctors now classify as disease.

## 10. Implications for the Chapman Argument

Chapman locates the construction of normality in statistical thinking (Quetelet c.1835, Galton c.1870s). This corpus analysis suggests that statistical normalisation formalised a norm that was already:

1. **Theologically grounded** (via media, 1660s–)
2. **Socially codified** (conduct literature, 1690s–)
3. **Medically pathologised** (nervous disorder literature, 1720s–)
4. **Fictionally dramatised** (sentimental/Burney novel, 1770s–)

The bell curve doesn't *invent* the normal — it inherits a normative behavioral subject that was constructed over 150 years through the institutional apparatus of the Anglican confessional state. The statistical turn is a late quantification of an exclusion that was already structurally embedded in theology, conduct prescription, medical diagnosis, and literary representation.

The key evidence: medical texts have **higher conformity vocabulary than conduct books** from 1700 onward. The doctors are already measuring deviation from a behavioral mean — they just don't have the mathematics yet. Cheyne's "English Malady" is a pre-statistical bell curve: a national nervous temperament from which individual pathology deviates.

## 11. Caveats

- **OCR quality:** Internet Archive texts have variable OCR quality; long-s artifacts may slightly distort word frequencies.
- **Corpus balance:** The fiction corpus is dominated by Burney (4 novels) and Young (2); other novelists would test whether Burney is typical or exceptional.
- **Category leakage:** Several conduct texts are written by physicians (Gregory, Cheyne); the medical/conduct boundary is porous by design — this is part of the argument, not a methodological flaw.
- **Keyword-in-context limitation:** This analysis counts words, not meanings. "Attention" in Locke means something different from "attention" in Gregory. Valence-aware analysis would strengthen the argument.
- **Missing texts:** No Methodism corpus (Wesley's journals/sermons), no Dissenting academy material, no parish/Poor Law records. These would fill the institutional enforcement layer.

## 12. Directions for Further Work

- **Valence analysis:** Do conduct texts use "sensibility" approvingly while medical texts use it diagnostically? A co-occurrence or collocate analysis would distinguish prescription from pathologisation.
- **Wesley and Methodism:** The anti-enthusiasm literature (Lavington) is here, but Wesley's own writings — the primary target — are not. The Methodist revival is the via media's great stress test.
- **Parish and Poor Law records:** Institutional enforcement of behavioral norms at the local level.
- **Expand the fiction corpus:** Fielding, Richardson, Smollett, Radcliffe, Austen — does the fiction-as-middle-ground pattern hold across the novel tradition or is it specific to Burney?
- **Topic modelling:** LDA or similar across the full 68-text corpus to discover latent theme clusters that keyword analysis may miss.
