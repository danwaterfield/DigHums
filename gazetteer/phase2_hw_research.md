# Phase 2 Canyon H/W Research Notes

## Methodology

H/W ratio = building height (ft) ÷ street width (ft).
Values > 1.0 = canyon effect (smoke/smell retained); < 1.0 = dispersed.

### Building heights from 1667 Rebuilding Act (confirmed from BHO statutes-realm/vol5/pp603-612)

| Grade | Storeys | Floor-to-ceiling heights | Estimated eaves height |
|-------|---------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| Grade 1 — high/principal streets | 4 | 10 + 10.5 + 9 + 8.5 = 38ft | ~45ft |
| Grade 2 — streets of note | 3 | 10 + 10 + 9 = 29ft | ~35ft |
| Grade 3 — by-lanes | 2 | 9 + 9 = 18ft | ~23ft |
| Courts/alleys | 1–2 | pre-Fire timber, often overhanging | ~18–20ft |

Minimum street width from Act: 24ft (previously < 14ft for many alleys).
1894 Building Act raised minimum to 40ft (not applicable to our period).

### Bath buildings (1774 Act context)
John Wood / Robert Adam Georgian terraces: typically 4 storeys above basement,
stone construction, floor heights similar to London Grade 1 = ~45ft eaves.

## Summary statistics
- Minimum H/W: 0.10 (open parks, Tyburn, rural)
- Maximum H/W: 3.50 (Garraway's, Exchange Alley)
- Median: ~1.20
- Key outliers HIGH: Garraway's (3.5), Chapter Coffee House (2.8), St Giles (2.5), Newgate/Old Bailey (2.0), Drury Lane Theatre (2.0)
- Key outliers LOW: Hyde Park (0.1), Tyburn (0.1), Portland Place (0.45), Vauxhall (0.15)
- Surprising findings: Russell Street coffeehouses (Will's, Button's, Tom's) = OPEN (0.7) — wide Covent Garden estate streets; Exchange Alley = extreme canyon despite City prestige
