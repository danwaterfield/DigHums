Method & Sources

What the figures count

Every number on this page is generated from the underlying CSVs and SQLite database at build time, not typed by hand, so the copy cannot drift ahead of the tools. “Novels” means texts that have been read into the evidence database — a subset of the wider plain-text corpus in the repository. “Passages” means evidence attached to a located venue; the database holds 13,669 passages in total, of which 5,766 are currently venue-linked and therefore mappable.

Limitations

Coverage is uneven. Of 167 gazetteer venues, 89 carry evidence; the rest are located but not yet documented. Narrative paths are only plotted for novels with at least five geolocated mentions, and thinly-attested novels produce correspondingly thin paths. Passage extraction is automated, so some quoted passages begin mid-sentence, and overlapping windows of the same sentence can appear as separate entries. Correspondence dates follow the source edition where a letter is undated, which pushes a small number of records outside the period.

Sources

Fiction from Project Gutenberg. Topography and diary evidence from British History Online (Survey of London; Old and New London), Strype’s Survey of London (1720), and published editions. Correspondence from the Hemlow Catalogue of the Burney Family Correspondence, published letter editions, and the correspSearch API. Bookseller data from Plomer’s trade directories. Historic map layers from Rocque (1746) and Horwood (1792–99) via Museum of London Archaeology / DHI; modern base map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Reuse
